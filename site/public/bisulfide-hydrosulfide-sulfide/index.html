Bisulfide, Hydrosulfide, and Sulfide

Three compound class names cluster around the HS¹¯/S²¯ chemistry: bisulfide and hydrosulfide both name the HS¹¯ class, while sulfide names the distinct S²¯ class. This page maps the relationships and distinctions between these three names.

Bisulfide = Hydrosulfide: Same Ion

Bisulfide and hydrosulfide are alternative names for the same compound class: both refer to compounds of the HS¹¯ ion.

  • Bisulfide (English) — most common in industrial contexts. German: Bisulfid.
  • Hydrosulfide (English) — common in academic chemistry. German: Hydrosulfid.
  • The HS¹¯ ion itself is called the bisulfide ion, hydrosulfide ion, hydrogen sulfide ion, or sulfanide ion.

Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) and sodium hydrosulfide (NaHS) are the same compound.

Sulfide: A Different Compound Class

Sulfide refers to a different compound class: S²¯ compounds. The S²¯ ion carries a double negative charge (−2) and contains no hydrogen. Key differences:

Property Bisulfide (HS⁻) Sulfide (S²⁻)
Ion formula HS¹¯ S²¯
Charge −1 −2
Contains hydrogen Yes (one H) No
Sodium salt formula NaHS Na&sub2;S
German name Bisulfid Sulfid

The Deprotonation Sequence

The three compound classes are connected through the deprotonation of hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S):

  • H&sub2;S → HS¹¯ + H¹+ (first deprotonation → bisulfide/hydrosulfide ion)
  • HS¹¯ → S²¯ + H¹+ (second deprotonation → sulfide ion)
