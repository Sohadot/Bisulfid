Compound Class · S²⁻ · English Form

Sulfide

Sulfide is the English-form name for compounds of the S²¯ ion — the sulfide dianion, a sulfur atom carrying a charge of −2. The German form is Sulfid. Sulfide is the most abundant inorganic sulfur compound class in the Earth’s crust, forming a large family of minerals.

The Sulfide Dianion

The S²¯ ion is formed by the complete deprotonation of hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S). It is the fully deprotonated form: both hydrogen atoms have been removed. The result is a sulfur atom with a charge of −2. For comparison, the bisulfide ion HS¹¯ is the half-deprotonated form — one hydrogen remains. See Sulfide Ion and HS¹¯ Ion for the ion-level distinction.

Sulfide Compounds

Sulfide compounds span ionic compounds and naturally occurring minerals:

  • Sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S) — two sodium ions per sulfide ion
  • Iron sulfide (FeS) — pyrrhotite mineral form
  • Zinc sulfide (ZnS) — sphalerite mineral / industrial form
  • Lead sulfide (PbS) — the mineral galena
  • Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) — technically an iron disulfide, often grouped with sulfide minerals

Sulfide vs Bisulfide: The Charge Difference

Sulfide (S²¯, charge −2) and bisulfide (HS¹¯, charge −1) are adjacent but distinct compound classes. The bisulfide ion retains one hydrogen and carries one fewer negative charge. This difference affects the stoichiometry of their salts: sodium sulfide is Na&sub2;S (two sodium ions), while sodium bisulfide is NaHS (one sodium ion).

The German-English Boundary

Sulfide (EN) and Sulfid (DE) differ by the final E. This is the same -id/-ide pattern that gives Bisulfid/bisulfide their paired names. See Language Boundary.
