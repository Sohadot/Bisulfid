Element · Sulfur · S (atomic number 16)

What is Sulfur?

Sulfur (chemical symbol S, atomic number 16) is a nonmetal element and the elemental foundation of the Bisulfid Atlas. Every compound on this reference surface — bisulfide, sulfide, disulfide, polysulfide — has sulfur as its defining element.

Sulfur in the Periodic Table

Sulfur is in Group 16 (the chalcogens) of the periodic table, directly below oxygen. Like oxygen, sulfur readily forms anions by gaining electrons: the sulfide ion S²¯ is formed when sulfur gains two electrons. This is the ion at the center of the sulfide compound class.

Sulfur Oxidation States

Sulfur has a wide range of oxidation states, from −2 to +6. The compound classes in this atlas map to different parts of this range:

  • S²¯ (sulfide, Sulfid): sulfur at −2
  • HS¹¯ (bisulfide, Bisulfid): sulfur at −2 (hydrogen-bearing form)
  • SO&sub3;²¯ (sulfite): sulfur at +4 — disambiguation node
  • SO&sub4;²¯ (sulfate, Sulfat): sulfur at +6 — disambiguation node

The German Word for Sulfur

In German, the element is called Schwefel, not Sulfur. However, compound names are built from the Latin root sulfur- in both languages. This is why the compound names share a root (Sulfid/sulfide, Bisulfid/bisulfide) while the element name itself differs between German and English.

Sulfur in Natural Occurrence

Sulfur occurs abundantly as sulfide minerals in the Earth’s crust: pyrite (FeS&sub2;), sphalerite (ZnS), and galena (PbS) are among the most common. Elemental sulfur occurs at volcanic sites and in evaporite deposits. This atlas covers sulfur in its compound form, not as elemental sulfur.
