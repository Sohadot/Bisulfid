Sulfuric Acid

Sulfuric acid (H&sub2;SO&sub4;) is a strong sulfur acid. It is not a sulfide compound. German: Schwefelsäure. This page draws the boundary between sulfuric acid and the sulfide/bisulfide compound family that bisulfid.com covers.

What Sulfuric Acid Is

Sulfuric acid has the formula H&sub2;SO&sub4;. It contains sulfur in the +6 oxidation state — the highest common oxidation state for sulfur. Sulfuric acid is a diprotic strong acid; losing both protons gives the sulfate ion (SO&sub4;²¯). It is one of the most industrially produced chemicals.

Sulfuric Acid vs the Sulfide Family

Sulfuric acid and the sulfide family (bisulfide, sulfide) represent opposite ends of the sulfur oxidation state range:

  • H&sub2;SO&sub4; (sulfuric acid): sulfur at +6 oxidation state; contains oxygen
  • H&sub2;S (hydrogen sulfide): sulfur at −2 oxidation state; no oxygen
  • HS¹¯ (bisulfide): sulfur at −2; no oxygen
  • S²¯ (sulfide): sulfur at −2; no oxygen

These are fundamentally different chemical species. Sulfuric acid is not a sulfide, not a bisulfide, and not related to the compound classes covered by bisulfid.com.

German: Schwefelsäure

The German name Schwefelsäure (“sulfur acid”) reflects the German naming convention for acids. It uses neither the -id nor the -at suffix, but the Säure (-säure) suffix for acids. This is distinct from the sulfate (Sulfat) and sulfide (Sulfid) compound class names.

The Sulfate/Acid Boundary

Sulfuric acid is the parent acid of the sulfate compound class. H&sub2;SO&sub4; → HSO&sub4;¹¯ (bisulfate) → SO&sub4;²¯ (sulfate/Sulfat). This is the entirely separate sulfate branch, covered in this atlas only as a boundary node. See Sulfat.
