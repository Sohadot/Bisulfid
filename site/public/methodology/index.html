Methodology · Reference Architecture · Claim Governance

Reference Methodology

bisulfid.com is a reference surface built around a specific thesis: the German-English suffix boundary in chemical nomenclature, centered on bisulfid/bisulfide. This page explains how the atlas is structured, what types of claims are made, and how sourcing and accuracy are approached.

What This Atlas Claims

The Bisulfid Atlas makes claims about terminology and naming conventions only. Specifically:

  • That German chemical nomenclature uses the -id suffix where English uses -ide
  • That bisulfid.com sits at this naming boundary, with the German form as the domain name
  • That Bisulfid (HS¹¯ compounds), Sulfid (S²¯ compounds), and related classes are distinct
  • That bisulfide and bisulfite are different compound classes with different chemistry

The atlas does not make market claims, medical claims, safety claims, or precise quantitative chemistry claims without explicit source locking.

Disambiguation as Core Architecture

A significant portion of this atlas is dedicated to drawing boundaries between compound classes that are commonly confused. The most important boundary is between bisulfide (HS¹¯) and bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯). These are completely different compound classes that happen to have similar-sounding names. bisulfid.com is not a bisulfite site, not a sulfite sequencing site, and not a generic sulfur acid site.

This disambiguation-first approach means the atlas explicitly names what it is not about, in order to make the boundaries of what it is about clear.

The Language Boundary as a Primary Axis

The central organizing principle of this atlas is the -id/-ide suffix difference between German and English chemical naming. All German -id form pages — Oxid, Hydroxid, Chlorid, and others — are here because they share this suffix pattern with Bisulfid and Sulfid. The axis is linguistic and nomenclatural, not purely chemical.

See Language Boundary for the full -id/-ide comparison, and The Missing E for the conceptual framing.

Sourcing and Claim Governance

Every factual claim on this surface is governed by a sourcing policy. Pages note where claims are based on observable conventions versus where they require verification against primary nomenclature authority documents. No claim is implied to be authoritative unless explicitly stated.

The Source Discipline page explains the full sourcing framework: what constitutes an approved claim, what constitutes an observable convention, and where the boundary lies.

What This Atlas Is Not

  • Not a safety manual or handling guide for any compound
  • Not a procurement catalog or product listing
  • Not a medical or toxicology reference
  • Not a bisulfite chemistry site or DNA sequencing reference
  • Not a competitor to IUPAC or primary chemistry databases

bisulfid.com occupies a specific niche: it explains the German-English naming boundary for the bisulfide/sulfide compound family and maps that boundary with precision.
