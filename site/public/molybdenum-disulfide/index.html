Molybdenum Disulfide (MoS₂)

Molybdenum disulfide (MoS&sub2;) is a layered transition metal sulfide. German: Molybdändisulfid. The formula MoS&sub2; indicates one molybdenum atom per two sulfide units. Despite the “disulfide” in the name, MoS&sub2; does not contain a disulfide bond — it contains two sulfide anions (S²¯) per molybdenum atom.

Naming Clarification

The name “molybdenum disulfide” uses the prefix di- to indicate two sulfur atoms per formula unit, not to indicate a sulfur–sulfur bond. In MoS&sub2;, each sulfur is an S²¯ ion; the two sulfur atoms are not directly bonded. This is different from a disulfide compound like pyrite (FeS&sub2;), where the two sulfur atoms form a disulfide anion (S&sub2;²¯) with a direct S–S bond.

Structure

MoS&sub2; has a layered structure: each layer consists of a plane of molybdenum atoms sandwiched between two planes of sulfur atoms. These layers are held together by weak van der Waals forces, which is why MoS&sub2; can serve as a solid lubricant — layers slide easily over one another. This layered structure is analogous to graphite.

MoS₂ in the Sulfide Compound Class

MoS&sub2; belongs to the sulfide compound class (containing S²¯ ions), not the disulfide class (S–S bond compounds). It is a molybdenum(IV) sulfide. See Sulfide and Disulfide for the compound class context.

German Name

In German, molybdenum disulfide is Molybdändisulfid. The German suffix follows the standard -id convention: Sulfid (not Sulfide). The di- prefix indicates two sulfide units.
