Nitrid

Nitrid is the German-form name for what English calls nitride. The final E is dropped: nitride (EN) becomes Nitrid (DE). Nitrid belongs to the pnictide group of German -id form compounds, alongside Phosphid (phosphide).

What Nitrid Names

Nitrid (nitride) compounds formally contain nitrogen in a negative oxidation state. The nitride anion N³¯ carries a charge of −3. Common examples include lithium nitride (Lithiumnitrid, Li&sub3;N), boron nitride (Bornitrid, BN — a refractory ceramic material), and silicon nitride (Siliciumnitrid, Si&sub3;N&sub4;). Ammonia (NH&sub3;) is the simplest nitrogen hydride rather than a nitride per se.

Nitrid in the -id Pattern

Nitrid sits in the pnictide group: elements of Group 15 that form binary compounds with negative oxidation states — Nitrid (N³¯), Phosphid (P³¯), and arsenide. All follow the German -id convention. See German Chemical Suffix for the full scope.
