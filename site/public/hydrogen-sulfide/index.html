Parent Compound · H₂S · Bisulfide/Sulfide Origin

Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S)

Hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S) is the parent compound from which both the bisulfide and sulfide compound classes are derived. It is a diprotic acid: losing one proton gives the bisulfide ion (HS¹¯); losing both protons gives the sulfide ion (S²¯). German name: Schwefelwasserstoff.

H₂S and the Bisulfide/Sulfide Family

The deprotonation sequence of H&sub2;S defines the compound class hierarchy:

  • H&sub2;S (hydrogen sulfide): two hydrogens; neutral
  • HS¹¯ (bisulfide / hydrosulfide ion): one hydrogen lost; charge −1
  • S²¯ (sulfide ion): both hydrogens lost; charge −2

This is why bisulfide and sulfide are adjacent compound classes — they differ by one deprotonation step from the same parent molecule.

The German Name

In German, hydrogen sulfide is Schwefelwasserstoff — literally “sulfur water substance”, following the German systematic name pattern for hydrides. This is distinct from the compound class names Bisulfid and Sulfid, which name the ionic compounds of the HS¹¯ and S²¯ ions respectively.

H₂S as a Reference Axis

In this atlas, hydrogen sulfide is referenced as the chemical origin of the bisulfide and sulfide compound families. The atlas does not cover hydrogen sulfide as a standalone chemistry topic or provide safety or handling information for it.
