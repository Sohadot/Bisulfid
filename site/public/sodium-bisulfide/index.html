Sodium Bisulfide (NaHS)

Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) is the sodium salt of the bisulfide ion (HS¹¯). It is an ionic compound of the bisulfide compound class. German: Natriumbisulfid (also Natriumhydrosulfid). Sodium bisulfide is a reference compound for the bisulfid.com atlas: it is the most directly relevant specific compound to the domain identity term.

Formula and Composition

Sodium bisulfide has the formula NaHS. It contains one sodium ion (Na¹+) per bisulfide ion (HS¹¯). The single positive charge of the sodium ion balances the single negative charge of the bisulfide anion. This 1:1 stoichiometry contrasts with sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S, 2:1 ratio) because the sulfide dianion carries a double negative charge.

German Names: Natriumbisulfid / Natriumhydrosulfid

In German, sodium bisulfide is Natriumbisulfid or Natriumhydrosulfid. Both names are used; they refer to the same compound (NaHS). Natriumbisulfid uses Bisulfid — the German -id form that is the domain identity term for bisulfid.com. Natriumhydrosulfid uses Hydrosulfid — the German -id form of the alternative name. See Hydrosulfide for the two-name context.

Sodium Bisulfide vs Sodium Sulfide

Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) and sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S) are different compounds:

  • NaHS: bisulfide ion (HS¹¯), charge −1, one sodium per formula unit
  • Na&sub2;S: sulfide ion (S²¯), charge −2, two sodium ions per formula unit

See Sodium Sulfide for the adjacent compound.
