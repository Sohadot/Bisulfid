German -id Form · Bromide · Halide Family

Bromid

Bromid is the German-form name for what English calls bromide. The final E is dropped: bromide (EN) becomes Bromid (DE). This follows the systematic German -id suffix convention seen in Bisulfid, Sulfid, Chlorid, Fluorid, and other compound class names.

What Bromid Names

Bromid (bromide) compounds contain the bromide anion Br¹¯. Bromine is a halogen, positioned between chlorine and iodine in Group 17 of the periodic table. Common bromide compounds include potassium bromide (Kaliumbromid, KBr) and sodium bromide (Natriumbromid, NaBr).

Bromid in the Halide Pattern

Bromid is part of the halide -id group alongside Fluorid, Chlorid, and Iodid. All four halide anions form German -id compound class names where English uses -ide. The pattern is part of the broader German -id convention. See German Chemical Suffix.
