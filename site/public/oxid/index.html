German -id Form · Oxide · DE/EN Boundary

Oxid

Oxid is the German-form name for what English calls oxide. The difference is the final letter E: English retains it (oxide), German drops it (Oxid). This is the same -id/-ide pattern that gives bisulfid.com its name: Bisulfid (DE) / bisulfide (EN).

What Oxid Names

Oxide compounds contain oxygen in a negative oxidation state, most commonly as the oxide dianion O²¯. Common oxide compounds include iron oxide (Eisenoxid), aluminum oxide (Aluminiumoxid), silicon dioxide (Siliciumdioxid), and water (Dihydrogenoxid / H&sub2;O). The German name for each uses the -id suffix rather than the -ide suffix.

Oxid in the -id Suffix Pattern

Oxid sits alongside Sulfid, Bisulfid, Chlorid, Hydroxid, and other German -id forms in the systematic pattern that drops the final E from compound class names. See German Chemical Suffix for the full pattern, and The Missing E for the central thesis.

Oxid vs Hydroxid

The oxide ion O²¯ and the hydroxide ion OH¹¯ are closely related but distinct. Hydroxid (German) / hydroxide (English) refers to the OH¹¯ compound class. Oxid is used for simple O²¯ compounds; Hydroxid is used for the OH¹¯ compounds.
