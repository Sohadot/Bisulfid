German -id Form · Disulfide · S–S Bond

Disulfid

Disulfid is the German-form name for what English calls disulfide. The final E is dropped: disulfide (EN) becomes Disulfid (DE). This follows the same -id/-ide suffix pattern as Bisulfid/bisulfide, Sulfid/sulfide, and all other German inorganic compound class names.

What Disulfid Names

Disulfid (disulfide) compounds contain a sulfur–sulfur (S–S) bond. This includes both inorganic ionic disulfides (such as pyrite, FeS&sub2;) and organic disulfides (R–S–S–R). The di- prefix indicates two sulfur units connected by a direct S–S bond.

Disulfid in the Sulfur Compound Family

Disulfid sits between Sulfid (S²¯) and Polysulfid (S⊂n²¯, n≥3) in the sulfur chain compound family:

  • Sulfid — single sulfur anion (S²¯)
  • Disulfid — two sulfur atoms bonded (S–S, S&sub2;²¯)
  • Polysulfid — three or more sulfur atoms in a chain

Disulfid vs Bisulfid

Disulfid and Bisulfid are different compound classes despite sharing the Bisulf-/Disulf- prefix structure:

  • Bisulfid (Bisulfid, HS¹¯): the hydrogen sulfide anion; bi- here refers to hydrogen-bearing or acid form
  • Disulfid (disulfide, S–S): the disulfur compound; di- here means “two sulfur atoms”
