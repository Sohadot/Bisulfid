German -id Form · Iodide · Halide Family

Iodid

Iodid is the German-form name for what English calls iodide. The final E is dropped in German: iodide (EN) becomes Iodid (DE). Iodid completes the German halide group alongside Fluorid, Chlorid, and Bromid.

What Iodid Names

Iodid (iodide) compounds contain the iodide anion I¹¯. Iodine is the heaviest of the common halogens. Common iodide compounds include potassium iodide (Kaliumiodid, KI) and sodium iodide (Natriumiodid, NaI).

The Halide -id Group Complete

With Iodid, the full halide group in German -id form is:

  • Fluorid — fluoride (F¹¯)
  • Chlorid — chloride (Cl¹¯)
  • Bromid — bromide (Br¹¯)
  • Iodid — iodide (I¹¯)

These four, plus the chalcogenides (Sulfid, Oxid) and other groups, make up the full scope of the German -id suffix convention.
