Compound Class · HS⁻ · English Form

Bisulfide

Bisulfide is the English-form name for the compound class defined by the HS¹¯ ion — the hydrogen sulfide anion, also called the hydrosulfide ion. The German form is Bisulfid — the domain identity term for bisulfid.com, where the final E is dropped following the German -id suffix convention.

What Bisulfide Compounds Are

Bisulfide compounds are ionic compounds containing the HS¹¯ anion as the anion component. The HS¹¯ ion is formed when hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S) loses one proton. It carries a single negative charge.

Common bisulfide compounds include:

  • Sodium bisulfide (NaHS)
  • Potassium bisulfide (KHS)
  • Ammonium bisulfide (NH&sub4;HS)

Bisulfide, Hydrosulfide, and Sulfide

Bisulfide and hydrosulfide are two names for the same HS¹¯ compound class. They are distinct from sulfide (S²¯ compounds), which involves complete deprotonation of H&sub2;S. See the disambiguation page for a full comparison.

Bisulfide vs Bisulfite: Critical Distinction

Bisulfide (HS¹¯) and bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯) are completely different compound classes. Bisulfite is a sulfite compound — it contains sulfur and oxygen. Bisulfide contains only sulfur and hydrogen in the anion. This distinction is architecturally important: bisulfid.com is a bisulfide site, not a bisulfite site.

The German-English Boundary

The name difference between bisulfide (EN) and Bisulfid (DE) is the subject of the canonical boundary page on this site. The domain name bisulfid.com holds the German form. See The Missing E for the conceptual framing.
