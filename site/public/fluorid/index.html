Fluorid

Fluorid is the German-form name for what English calls fluoride. The final E is dropped in the German form, following the systematic -id/-ide suffix pattern that runs through all German inorganic ionic compound names.

What Fluorid Names

Fluorid (fluoride) compounds contain the fluoride anion F¹¯. Fluorine is the most electronegative element and the lightest halogen. Common fluoride compounds include sodium fluoride (Natriumfluorid, NaF), calcium fluoride (Calciumfluorid, CaF&sub2; — the mineral fluorite), and hydrogen fluoride (Fluorwasserstoff, HF).

Fluorid in the Halide Family

Fluorid belongs to the halide group of -id form compounds. The four halide -id forms in German are: Fluorid, Chlorid, Bromid, and Iodid. All follow the -id suffix convention where English uses -ide.
