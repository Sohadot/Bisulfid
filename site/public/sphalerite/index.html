Sphalerite (ZnS)

Sphalerite is the mineral form of zinc sulfide (ZnS). It is a sulfide mineral — a member of the sulfide compound class. German: Sphalerit or Zinkblende. Sphalerite is the primary natural source of zinc and the most common zinc mineral.

Composition

The ideal formula for sphalerite is ZnS — one zinc cation (Zn²+) per sulfide anion (S²¯). In practice, natural sphalerite typically contains iron as a minor impurity, giving a composition of (Zn,Fe)S. Iron content can range from trace amounts to several percent of the formula unit. The presence of iron generally darkens the mineral from nearly colorless or yellow to dark brown or black.

Sphalerite and the Sulfide Compound Class

Sphalerite is one of the most structurally important sulfide minerals. The “sphalerite structure” (also called zinc blende structure) is a reference crystal structure in inorganic chemistry, used to describe the bonding arrangement in ZnS and in many other compounds with the same general structure (e.g., GaAs, InP).

German: Sphalerit / Zinkblende

In German mineralogy, sphalerite is called Sphalerit or Zinkblende (zinc blende). The compound zinc sulfide is Zinksulfid. The mineral name Zinkblende (“zinc blind”) refers to the mineral’s non-metallic appearance despite its metallic zinc content.

Sulfide Mineral Family

Sphalerite belongs to the same sulfide mineral family as:

  • Galena (PbS) — lead sulfide mineral
  • Pyrrhotite (FeS) — iron sulfide mineral
  • Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) — iron disulfide mineral (different compound class)
