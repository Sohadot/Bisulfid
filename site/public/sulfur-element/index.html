Sulfur

Sulfur (chemical symbol S, atomic number 16) is the element from which the entire Bisulfid Atlas is built. Every compound in this atlas — bisulfide, sulfide, disulfide, polysulfide, thiosulfate — has sulfur as a defining component. This page provides element context for the atlas, not a full sulfur chemistry reference.

Sulfur in the Compound Family

Sulfur is a nonmetal in Group 16 of the periodic table. In its role as an anion-forming element, sulfur takes on negative oxidation states that define the compound classes in this atlas:

  • Sulfur in the −2 oxidation state gives the sulfide dianion S²¯ — the defining ion of sulfide / Sulfid compounds
  • Sulfur in HS¹¯ gives the bisulfide/hydrosulfide ion — sulfur bonded to one hydrogen, carrying a single negative charge
  • Sulfur–sulfur (S–S) bonds form disulfide compounds and polysulfide chains

Sulfur in German and English

The element name itself is “Schwefel” in German, not “Sulfur” — but compound names are built from the Latin root sulfur- in both languages. This is why the compound names share a common stem while differing in suffix: Bisulfid (DE) / bisulfide (EN), Sulfid (DE) / sulfide (EN).

Sulfur Compound Classes in This Atlas

The Bisulfid Atlas covers sulfur specifically in its role as the central element in:

  • Bisulfide / Bisulfid — HS¹¯ compounds
  • Sulfide / Sulfid — S²¯ compounds
  • Hydrosulfide — HS¹¯ (alternative naming)
  • Disulfide — S–S bond compounds
  • Polysulfide — S⊂n²¯ chain compounds
  • Thiosulfate — S&sub2;O&sub3;²¯ compounds

Adjacent Sulfur Compounds (Disambiguation)

Several important sulfur compound classes are adjacent to the sulfide family but outside the scope of this atlas:

  • Bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯) and sulfite (SO&sub3;²¯) — sulfur-oxygen anions, not sulfides
  • Sulfat / sulfate (SO&sub4;²¯) — fully oxidized sulfur anion
  • Sulfuric acid (H&sub2;SO&sub4;) — the most common strong sulfur acid; not a sulfide compound
