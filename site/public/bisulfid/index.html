Bisulfid

Bisulfid is the German-form spelling of bisulfide — the compound class defined by the HS¹¯ ion (hydrogen sulfide anion). Where English writes bisulfide, German writes Bisulfid — dropping the final E following the systematic German -id suffix convention. This term is the domain identity of bisulfid.com: the site is named after the German form.

The Bisulfid Compound Class

Bisulfid refers to compounds of the HS¹¯ ion. This ion is also called the hydrogen sulfide anion or the hydrosulfide ion. It forms when hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S) loses one proton. Common Bisulfid-class compounds include sodium bisulfide (Natriumbisulfid, NaHS) and potassium bisulfide (Kaliumbisulfid, KHS).

In German-language chemistry, Bisulfid and Sulfid name adjacent compound classes: Bisulfid for HS¹¯ compounds, Sulfid for S²¯ compounds. The English forms bisulfide and sulfide parallel these exactly.

Why This Term Defines the Domain

bisulfid.com is named after Bisulfid — the German form — because the site sits at the exact boundary between German and English chemical naming conventions. The -id/-ide suffix difference is systematic and documented. The domain name embeds the boundary it describes. See Bisulfid vs Bisulfide for the canonical comparison, and The Missing E for the conceptual framing.

Bisulfid, Sulfid, and Hydrosulfid

Three German-form terms name closely related compound classes:

  • Bisulfid (this page) — HS¹¯ compounds
  • Sulfid — S²¯ compounds
  • Hydrosulfid — alternative name for HS¹¯ compounds (same as Bisulfid)

See the disambiguation page for the three-way comparison.

Not to Confuse: Bisulfit

Bisulfit (German) or bisulfite (English) — HSO&sub3;¹¯ — is a completely different compound class. It is a sulfite compound, not a sulfide compound. bisulfid.com is not a bisulfite site. See Bisulfite for the disambiguation.
