Nomenclature · DE/EN Suffix Pattern · Central Thesis

The Missing E

German chemical names for inorganic compound classes systematically drop the final letter E that appears in the corresponding English names. Where English writes bisulfide, German writes Bisulfid. Where English writes oxide, German writes Oxid. This single-letter difference — the Missing E — is the central axis of bisulfid.com.

The Pattern Across Compound Classes

The -id/-ide suffix difference is not unique to bisulfid/bisulfide. It runs systematically across German inorganic chemistry:

Why This Difference Exists

The -id/-ide distinction is a property of the two languages’ nomenclature traditions, not a chemical difference. The compounds are identical — only the language changes. Sodium bisulfide in English is Natriumbisulfid in German; they refer to the same substance (NaHS).

German inorganic nomenclature uses the -id suffix as a terminal form. English adopted the -ide form, which adds the E. This divergence applies consistently across the halide, chalcogenide, pnictide, and carbide families. See German Chemical Suffix for a fuller treatment of the pattern.

The Missing E and bisulfid.com

bisulfid.com holds the German form of bisulfide as its domain name. The E is missing from the domain — deliberately, because the missing E is the thesis. The domain name embeds the language boundary it describes.

English speakers searching for bisulfide will find bisulfid.com at the boundary. German speakers or those familiar with German chemical naming will recognize the -id form immediately. The atlas maps both sides of this boundary.

See Bisulfid vs Bisulfide for the canonical explanation of this boundary.

What the Missing E Is Not

The -id/-ide suffix difference applies to the -ide compound class suffix. It does not apply to:

  • Bisulfite — German: Bisulfit (the -it suffix is a different convention for sulfite compounds). See Bisulfite disambiguation.
  • Sulfate — German: Sulfat (the -at/-ate suffix applies to oxyanion salts, not to the same suffix pattern). See Sulfat.
  • Proper names, trade names, or brand names, which do not follow this convention.
