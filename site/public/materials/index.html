Sulfur-Based Materials

Sulfur compounds occur both as natural minerals and as produced industrial chemicals. This page indexes the specific materials covered in the Bisulfid Atlas — compounds that belong to the sulfide family and are referenced here as part of the compound class mapping, not as product listings or procurement guides.

Natural Sulfide Minerals

Sulfide minerals are among the most common ore minerals in the Earth’s crust. They form when metal cations bond with sulfide (S²¯) anions in geological environments. The bisulfid.com atlas covers several important sulfide minerals as named reference nodes:

  • Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) — Iron disulfide, the most abundant sulfide mineral. Also called “fool’s gold” for its metallic appearance. Strictly an iron disulfide, not a simple iron sulfide.
  • Sphalerite (ZnS) — Zinc sulfide mineral, the primary natural source of zinc. The mineral form of the zinc sulfide compound class.
  • Galena (PbS) — Lead sulfide mineral, the primary natural source of lead. Historically one of the most important ore minerals.
  • Iron Sulfide (FeS, FeS&sub2;) — Covers the iron sulfide mineral family including pyrrhotite (FeS) and pyrite (FeS&sub2;).

Industrial Sulfide Compounds

Several sulfide compounds are produced industrially and serve as reference examples for the compound classes in this atlas:

  • Sodium Bisulfide (NaHS) — A bisulfide-class compound used in industrial processes. Sodium salt of the HS¹¯ ion.
  • Sodium Sulfide (Na&sub2;S) — A sulfide-class compound. Sodium salt of the S²¯ ion.
  • Zinc Sulfide (ZnS) — Both a natural mineral (sphalerite) and an industrial material with optical properties.
  • Molybdenum Disulfide (MoS&sub2;) — A layered sulfide material used as a solid lubricant. Not a bisulfide — the name refers to two sulfide units per formula unit, not a disulfide bond.

Parent and Related Compounds

  • Hydrogen Sulfide (H&sub2;S) — The parent compound of the bisulfide and sulfide chemistry. Losing one proton from H&sub2;S gives HS¹¯ (bisulfide); losing two gives S²¯ (sulfide).
  • Thiols (R–SH) — Organic analogs of hydrogen sulfide; boundary with the sulfide family.

What This Atlas Does Not Cover

This materials page covers sulfide compounds. It does not cover:

  • Sulfate minerals (gypsum, barite) — these are sulfate compounds, not sulfides. See Sulfat.
  • Sulfite compounds — see Sulfite and Bisulfite for disambiguation.
  • Elemental sulfur, sulfur oxides, or sulfuric acid — adjacent chemistry not within the sulfide compound scope.
