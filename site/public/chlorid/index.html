German -id Form · Chloride · Halide Family

Chlorid

Chlorid is the German-form name for what English calls chloride. German drops the final E: chloride (EN) becomes Chlorid (DE). This is the same -id/-ide suffix difference that gives Bisulfid (bisulfide), Sulfid (sulfide), and Oxid (oxide) their German forms.

What Chlorid Names

Chlorid (chloride) compounds contain the chloride anion Cl¹¯. Chloride is a halide — the anion formed from chlorine. Common chloride compounds include sodium chloride (Natriumchlorid, NaCl — common salt), potassium chloride (Kaliumchlorid, KCl), and hydrogen chloride (Chlorwasserstoff, HCl).

Chlorid in the Halide Family

The halide anions (Cl¹¯, F¹¯, Br¹¯, I¹¯) all use the -id suffix in German:

  • Chlorid — chloride (Cl¹¯)
  • Fluorid — fluoride (F¹¯)
  • Bromid — bromide (Br¹¯)
  • Iodid — iodide (I¹¯)

This halide group, plus the chalcogenides (Sulfid, Oxid) and other binary compound classes, form the full scope of the German -id suffix convention. See German Chemical Suffix for the full pattern.

Chlorid and the Bisulfid Atlas

Chlorid is included in this atlas not as a focus topic but as an illustrative member of the -id suffix family. The atlas centers on the sulfide/bisulfide family and uses Chlorid to demonstrate that the -id/-ide naming difference is systematic across German inorganic chemistry, not specific to sulfur compounds.
