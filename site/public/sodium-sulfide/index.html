Sodium Sulfide (Na₂S)

Sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S) is the sodium salt of the sulfide dianion (S²¯). It is an ionic compound of the sulfide class. German: Natriumsulfid. Sodium sulfide is distinct from sodium bisulfide (NaHS), which is the sodium salt of the bisulfide ion (HS¹¯).

Formula and Composition

Sodium sulfide has the formula Na&sub2;S. It contains two sodium ions (Na¹+) per sulfide ion (S²¯). The two positive charges of the sodium ions balance the −2 charge of the sulfide dianion. This stoichiometry (2:1) contrasts with sodium bisulfide (NaHS, 1:1 ratio) because the bisulfide ion carries only a single negative charge.

Sodium Sulfide vs Sodium Bisulfide

The difference between Na&sub2;S and NaHS illustrates the core distinction between the sulfide and bisulfide compound classes:

  • Na&sub2;S (sodium sulfide) — sodium salt of S²¯. Two sodium ions per formula unit.
  • NaHS (sodium bisulfide) — sodium salt of HS¹¯. One sodium ion per formula unit.

In solution, these two compounds behave differently due to the different proton counts of their anions.

German Name: Natriumsulfid

In German, sodium sulfide is Natriumsulfid — combining the German name for sodium (Natrium) with the German -id form of sulfide (Sulfid). This follows the standard German inorganic compound naming convention.
