Ion Reference · Bisulfide Class · HS⁻

The HS⁻ Ion

The HS¹¯ ion — also written HS⁻ — is the hydrogen sulfide anion. It is the defining ion of the bisulfide compound class. In German-language chemistry it is associated with Bisulfid; in English-language chemistry it is associated with bisulfide. It is also called the hydrosulfide ion or the sulfanide ion.

Formation

The HS¹¯ ion is formed when hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S) undergoes the first step of deprotonation — losing one of its two hydrogen atoms as a proton (H¹+):

H&sub2;S → HS¹¯ + H¹+

If H&sub2;S loses a second proton, the result is the sulfide dianion S²¯, which is the defining ion of the sulfide compound class (German: Sulfid). The two ions are therefore related through sequential deprotonation of the same parent molecule.

Names for HS⁻

The HS¹¯ ion has several names in current use:

  • Bisulfide ion (or bisulfide anion) — the most common name in industrial and English-language contexts
  • Hydrogen sulfide ion — a systematic name based on the parent H&sub2;S
  • Hydrosulfide ion (or hydrosulfide anion) — common in academic chemistry; see Hydrosulfide
  • Sulfanide ion — a more formal IUPAC-adjacent name

The German-language equivalents are Bisulfid-Ion or Hydrosulfid-Ion. All names refer to the same species: HS¹¯.

Compounds of HS⁻

Ionic compounds of HS¹¯ include:

  • Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) — sodium salt of HS¹¯
  • Potassium bisulfide (KHS) — potassium salt of HS¹¯
  • Ammonium bisulfide (NH&sub4;HS) — ammonium salt of HS¹¯
  • Calcium bisulfide (Ca(HS)&sub2;) — calcium salt

HS⁻ vs S²⁻

The bisulfide ion HS¹¯ and the sulfide dianion S²¯ are distinct species. They carry different charges, have different formulas, and form different compound classes. The disambiguation page covers the three-way relationship between bisulfide (HS¹¯), hydrosulfide (HS¹¯, same ion), and sulfide (S²¯).

What HS⁻ Is Not

HS¹¯ should not be confused with:

  • HSO&sub3;¹¯ (bisulfite ion) — a sulfite compound, not a sulfide. See Bisulfite.
  • SO&sub3;²¯ (sulfite ion) — see Sulfite.
  • S²¯ (sulfide dianion) — see Sulfide Ion.
