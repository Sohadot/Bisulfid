German -id Form · Sulfide · S²⁻ Class

Sulfid

Sulfid is the German-form name for what English calls sulfide. German drops the final E: sulfide (EN) becomes Sulfid (DE). This is the same -id/-ide suffix difference that gives Bisulfid/bisulfide their paired names. Sulfid names the S²¯ compound class — the sulfide dianion compounds.

What Sulfid Names

Sulfid (sulfide) compounds contain the sulfide dianion S²¯ — a sulfur atom carrying a charge of −2. Common sulfide compounds include:

  • Natriumsulfid (Na&sub2;S) — sodium sulfide
  • Eisensulfid (FeS) — iron sulfide (pyrrhotite)
  • Zinksulfid (ZnS) — zinc sulfide (sphalerite)
  • Bleisulfid (PbS) — lead sulfide (galena)

Sulfid vs Bisulfid

Sulfid (S²¯, charge −2) and Bisulfid (HS¹¯, charge −1) are adjacent but distinct compound classes. Bisulfid retains one hydrogen atom and carries half the negative charge of Sulfid. The bisulfid.com domain sits at the naming boundary of the Bisulfid compound class specifically; Sulfid is the adjacent class.

Sulfid vs Sulfit

Sulfid (sulfide, S²¯) should not be confused with Sulfit (sulfite, SO&sub3;²¯). The -id vs -it suffix difference in German marks two completely different compound classes. See Sulfite and Sulfat for disambiguation.

The -id Suffix Pattern

The Sulfid -id suffix is the same suffix used in Bisulfid, Oxid, Chlorid, Hydroxid, and all other German ionic compound class names. See German Chemical Suffix for the full scope.
