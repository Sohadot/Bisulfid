1. Purpose of this draft

This file exists to align planned English copy with controlled German-English chemical terminology relevant to Bisulfid and related sulfur compound naming. It is a terminology-control draft - a working inventory and cautious framing - not a formal nomenclature standard, textbook, or regulatory summary.

Any factual alignment between German lexical forms and English chemical forms on this page is presented for internal draft review until stronger sources are locked. This page must not be read as establishing publication readiness for the german_english_chemical_terms route.

3. German lexical forms under review

German chemical names for many binary compounds commonly use -id where English often uses -ide for a cognate term [SOURCE REQUIRED]. German lexical usage in dictionaries and general chemical discourse is not the same thing as a formal nomenclature authority citation [SOURCE REQUIRED].

Examples held for candidate cross-reference only (not verified for public statement):

  • Sulfid (DE term under teaching/lexical review) [SOURCE REQUIRED]
  • Oxid, Chlorid, Disulfid (same framing) [SOURCE REQUIRED]

4. English chemical forms under review

English forms such as sulfide, oxide, chloride, disulfide are widely used in English-language chemistry. How each pair maps to a specific German standard name, including bisulfide vs bisulfid and hydrosulfide nuances, requires per-term source-locking [SOURCE REQUIRED]. This draft does not treat any English form as automatically paired with a single approved German string for publication.

5. Where German and English terminology diverge

Spelling, suffix choice, and ion naming can diverge across languages and contexts (teaching slides, industrial SDS language, IUPAC systematic names, common names). Draft review assumption only: apparent parallels (for example -id vs -ide) need case-by-case verification [SOURCE REQUIRED].

Important: This draft does not assert a universal English-to-German suffix transformation rule as approved fact. Any general pattern remains candidate support pending nomenclature and lexical sources - not a rule cleared for public pages.

6. Academic teaching support versus formal authority

Academic teaching materials (for example university course notes) may illustrate how terms are introduced in class. That candidate support does not establish:

  • DIN (or successor) standard text as binding for this site [SOURCE REQUIRED]
  • Official German-language IUPAC or national-body authority for every term pair listed here [SOURCE REQUIRED]

Formal nomenclature authority and German lexical evidence are different evidence classes. Conflating them would overstate what teaching PDFs can prove. Source-locking required before any language on this page is treated as publication-grade.
