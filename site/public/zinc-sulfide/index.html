Zinc Sulfide (ZnS)

Zinc sulfide (ZnS) is a compound of zinc and the sulfide ion (S²¯). It belongs to the sulfide compound class. German: Zinksulfid. Zinc sulfide occurs naturally as the mineral sphalerite and has industrial applications based on its optical properties.

Formula and Structure

Zinc sulfide has the formula ZnS — one zinc ion (Zn²+) per sulfide ion (S²¯). The two charges balance directly (Zn²+ + S²¯ = neutral compound). ZnS crystallizes in two main structures: the sphalerite (zinc blende) structure and the wurtzite structure, both tetrahedrally coordinated.

Natural Form: Sphalerite

Sphalerite (ZnS) is the most important zinc ore mineral. It typically contains iron as a minor impurity (Zn,Fe)S, which can affect its color. Pure ZnS is nearly colorless; iron-bearing sphalerite is commonly yellowish to dark brown. Sphalerite is the primary source of zinc metal.

German Name: Zinksulfid

The German name Zinksulfid combines Zink (zinc) with Sulfid — the German -id form of sulfide. This follows the standard German inorganic compound naming convention. See Sulfid for the compound class context.
