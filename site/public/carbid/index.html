Carbid

Carbid is the German-form name for what English calls carbide. The final E is dropped: carbide (EN) becomes Carbid (DE). Carbid follows the same German -id suffix convention as Sulfid, Oxid, Nitrid, and other compound class names.

What Carbid Names

Carbid (carbide) compounds contain carbon in a negative oxidation state or in binary compounds with metals or other elements. The term covers a range of compound types with different anion structures. Common examples include calcium carbide (Calciumcarbid, CaC&sub2;), silicon carbide (Siliciumcarbid, SiC — an industrial abrasive and refractory material), and tungsten carbide (Wolframcarbid, WC — used in cutting tools).

Carbid in the -id Pattern

Carbid illustrates how the German -id suffix applies to a compound class where the “anion” is carbon-based rather than a simple monatomic species. The same convention applies to Nitrid (nitride) and Phosphid (phosphide). See German Chemical Suffix for the full scope.
