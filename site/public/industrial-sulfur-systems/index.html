Industrial Sulfur Systems

Industrial sulfur systems involve a range of sulfur compound classes. This page maps the compound class context — the naming and chemistry of the sulfur compounds that appear in industrial sulfur contexts. This atlas covers the compound names and classes; it does not make performance claims or procurement recommendations.

Compound Classes in Industrial Sulfur Contexts

Industrial sulfur chemistry involves several distinct compound classes. Each class is defined by its ion or bond type:

  • Sulfide (S²¯) — Binary sulfur compounds. Includes sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S), iron sulfide (FeS), and zinc sulfide (ZnS). German: Sulfid.
  • Bisulfide (HS¹¯) — The hydrogen-bearing sulfur anion. Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) is the reference industrial compound. German: Bisulfid.
  • Polysulfide (S⁻⁻) — Chain sulfur compounds with multiple sulfur atoms. German: Polysulfid.
  • Thiosulfate (S&sub2;O&sub3;²¯) — Mixed sulfur-oxygen compound class used in various industrial processes.
  • Sulfuric Acid (H&sub2;SO&sub4;) — The most produced industrial chemical; parent acid of the sulfate class.

Bisulfide in Industrial Sulfur Systems

Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) is the most common industrial bisulfide compound. It is an ionic salt of the bisulfide ion (HS¹¯). See Sodium Bisulfide for compound class details. The bisulfid.com atlas uses sodium bisulfide as its reference compound for the bisulfide class.

Naming Context: Bisulfide vs Bisulfite

Two compound class names that are frequently confused in industrial contexts:

  • Bisulfide (HS¹¯) — hydrogen sulfur anion; no oxygen.
  • Bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯) — hydrogen sulfite oxyanion; contains oxygen. bisulfid.com is a bisulfide reference site, not a bisulfite site.

The spelling difference (“-ide” vs “-ite”) marks a chemical class boundary, not a variant spelling of the same thing.

Hydrogen Sulfide as Parent Compound

Hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S) is the parent acid of the sulfide and bisulfide compound classes. Deprotonation of H&sub2;S produces the bisulfide ion (HS¹¯); a second deprotonation produces the sulfide ion (S²¯). This deprotonation sequence connects the compound classes in industrial sulfur contexts.
