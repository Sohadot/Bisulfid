Sulfat

Sulfat is the German name for sulfate (SO&sub4;²¯). It uses the -at suffix, not the -id suffix. Sulfat is not the same compound class as Sulfid. This page exists to draw the boundary between German sulfate (Sulfat) and German sulfide (Sulfid) terminology.

Sulfat vs Sulfid: Two Different Suffixes, Two Different Compounds

In German chemical nomenclature:

  • Sulfid (DE) = sulfide (EN): the S²¯ compound class. Uses the -id suffix.
  • Sulfat (DE) = sulfate (EN): the SO&sub4;²¯ compound class. Uses the -at suffix.

The -id suffix applies to simple binary anion compounds (sulfide, oxide, chloride, etc.). The -at suffix applies to oxyanion salts (sulfate, carbonate, nitrate, etc.). These are two distinct conventions in German inorganic nomenclature.

What Sulfate Is

The sulfate ion (SO&sub4;²¯) contains one sulfur atom bonded to four oxygen atoms, carrying a charge of −2. Sulfur in sulfate is in the +6 oxidation state — the highest common oxidation state for sulfur. Common sulfate compounds include sodium sulfate (Natriumsulfat, Na&sub2;SO&sub4;), calcium sulfate (Calciumsulfat, CaSO&sub4; — gypsum), and magnesium sulfate (Magnesiumsulfat, MgSO&sub4;).

Sulfat and the -at/-id Boundary

The -at suffix in German covers the -ate oxyanion family: Sulfat (sulfate), Carbonat (carbonate), Nitrat (nitrate), Phosphat (phosphate), and others. These are a different compound class from the -id compounds (Sulfid, Oxid, Chlorid). The Bisulfid Atlas covers the -id compound family; Sulfat appears here only as a disambiguation node at the edge of that scope. See German Chemical Suffix for the broader suffix context.
