Governance · Source Discipline

Source Discipline

bisulfid.com operates under a strict source discipline principle: no claim reaches a public page without a corresponding verified source.

The Governing Principle

Every factual claim that appears on a public-facing page of bisulfid.com must be supported by a traceable, credentialled source. If no verified source exists for a claim, that claim does not appear on the public surface.

This means the public site may contain less content than readers expect at any given time. That is intentional. Accuracy and source integrity take precedence over completeness.

Source Categories

The source framework is designed to accept the following source types:

  • Chemical nomenclature authorities — recognised bodies that govern compound naming conventions, including IUPAC and DIN.
  • Scientific literature — peer-reviewed publications where relevant to terminology and nomenclature claims.
  • Industrial reference databases — verified industry data relevant to sulfur compounds and their applications.
  • Regulatory bodies — official sources where regulatory framing is relevant.

What Is Permanently Blocked

The following are permanently outside the scope of bisulfid.com, regardless of source availability:

  • Safety thresholds, handling instructions, or exposure guidance of any kind
  • Medical or therapeutic claims
  • Bisulfite data presented as bisulfide data (the two compounds are distinct)
  • AI-generated summaries as primary sources
  • Unsourced numerical market claims

Source Discipline as Public Trust

Source discipline is not just an internal governance mechanism. It is a public trust principle. When bisulfid.com publishes a claim, that claim has a source. When a source cannot be verified, the claim is held back.

The site does not present speculative or unverified claims as facts, and does not use rhetorical framing to imply authority it has not earned through source verification.
