Sulfid vs Sulfide

Sulfid (German) and sulfide (English) name the same compound class: compounds of the S²¯ ion. The only difference is the final letter E — German drops it, English keeps it. This is the same -id/-ide suffix boundary at the heart of bisulfid.com (Bisulfid vs bisulfide), extended here to the adjacent sulfide compound class.

Same Compound Class, Two Language Forms

Both terms refer to S²¯ compounds. Natriumsulfid (DE) and sodium sulfide (EN) are the same compound (Na&sub2;S). Zinksulfid (DE) and zinc sulfide (EN) are the same compound (ZnS). The chemical identity is unchanged; only the language changes.

The Suffix Difference

German uses the -id suffix for ionic compound classes. English uses the -ide suffix. This is the same difference that gives:

  • Bisulfid (DE) / bisulfide (EN)
  • Sulfid (DE) / sulfide (EN)
  • Oxid (DE) / oxide (EN)
  • Chlorid (DE) / chloride (EN)

See The Missing E for the full conceptual framing and German Chemical Suffix for the full scope.

Sulfid/Sulfide vs Bisulfid/Bisulfide

Sulfid/sulfide and Bisulfid/bisulfide are adjacent but distinct compound classes:

  • Sulfid / sulfide: S²¯ (charge −2, no hydrogen)
  • Bisulfid / bisulfide: HS¹¯ (charge −1, one hydrogen)

For the full comparison, see Bisulfide, Hydrosulfide, Sulfide.
