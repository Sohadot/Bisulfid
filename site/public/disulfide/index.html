Disulfide

Disulfide compounds contain a sulfur–sulfur (S–S) bond. The German form is Disulfid. The disulfide bond is found in inorganic ionic compounds and in organic molecules, where it plays structural and biochemical roles. Disulfide is distinct from bisulfide: bisulfide refers to HS¹¯ compounds; disulfide refers to compounds with a S–S bond.

Inorganic Disulfides

In inorganic chemistry, the disulfide (or persulfide) anion S&sub2;²¯ contains a sulfur–sulfur bond. Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) is the most common natural example — it is formally an iron(II) disulfide, not iron(II) sulfide. See Pyrite for the mineral context.

Organic Disulfides

In organic chemistry, the disulfide linkage R–S–S–R connects two organosulfur groups through a sulfur–sulfur bond. This bond is important in protein chemistry, where it helps maintain three-dimensional protein structure. Disulfide bonds in biochemistry are covered on a dedicated atlas page.

Molybdenum Disulfide

Molybdenum disulfide (MoS&sub2;) is named for having two sulfide units per formula unit (di + sulfide), not for containing a disulfide bond. It is a layered sulfide material used as a solid lubricant. The naming can be a source of confusion.

The German Form: Disulfid

The German name Disulfid drops the final E from disulfide, following the same -id/-ide pattern as all other German -id form compound names.
