Thiosulfate

Thiosulfate (S&sub2;O&sub3;²¯) is a sulfur compound that contains both sulfur and oxygen. German: Thiosulfat. Thiosulfate is distinct from sulfide and distinct from bisulfide. It appears in this atlas as a boundary node to clarify the scope of the sulfide compound family.

Thiosulfate Ion

The thiosulfate ion S&sub2;O&sub3;²¯ can be thought of as a sulfate ion (SO&sub4;²¯) in which one oxygen has been replaced by a sulfur atom. It contains two sulfur atoms: one in a +5 oxidation state and one in a −1 state (the exact oxidation state assignment depends on the bonding model used). The overall charge is −2.

Common Thiosulfate Compounds

The most common thiosulfate compound is sodium thiosulfate (Na&sub2;S&sub2;O&sub3;), also known as “hypo” in photographic processing contexts. Sodium thiosulfate is used in various analytical and industrial processes.

Thiosulfate vs Sulfide: The Boundary

Thiosulfate and sulfide are both sulfur-containing anions, but they are different compound classes:

  • Sulfide (S²¯): sulfur only, oxidation state −2
  • Thiosulfate (S&sub2;O&sub3;²¯): two sulfur atoms plus three oxygens

The prefix thio- in thiosulfate indicates the substitution of sulfur for oxygen in the parent oxyanion (sulfate). This is a systematic prefix in sulfur chemistry, not a name for the sulfide compound class.

German: Thiosulfat

The German name Thiosulfat uses the -at suffix, following the German convention for oxyanion sulfur compounds. Compare Sulfat (sulfate) and Sulfit (sulfite) — all use the -at/-it suffix rather than the -id suffix reserved for simple binary anion compounds like Sulfid and Bisulfid.
