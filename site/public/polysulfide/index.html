Compound Class · S⊂n²⁻ · Sulfur Chains

Polysulfide

Polysulfide compounds contain a chain of sulfur atoms as the anion, written S⊂n²¯ where n is 3 or more. The German form is Polysulfid. Polysulfides extend the sulfide compound family into multi-atom sulfur chains, sitting above disulfide (n=2) and sulfide (n=1) in complexity.

Polysulfide Anions

Polysulfide anions have the general formula S⊂n²¯, where n is typically 3–6. Common examples include:

  • S&sub3;²¯ (trisulfide ion)
  • S&sub4;²¯ (tetrasulfide ion)
  • S&sub5;²¯ (pentasulfide ion)

These anions consist of a chain of sulfur atoms with a total charge of −2, typically carried on the terminal sulfur atoms.

Polysulfide Compounds

Ionic polysulfide compounds include sodium polysulfide (Na&sub2;S⊂n;) and potassium polysulfide (K&sub2;S⊂n;). Polysulfide minerals occur naturally in sulfur-rich geological environments.

The German Form: Polysulfid

The German name Polysulfid follows the -id convention. Like Bisulfid, Sulfid, and Disulfid, the German form drops the final E from the English -ide suffix.
