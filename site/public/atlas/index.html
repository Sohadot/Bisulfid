The Bisulfid Atlas

The Bisulfid Atlas is a systematic reference surface for sulfur compound nomenclature, built around the boundary between German and English chemical naming conventions. The central axis is the -id/-ide suffix difference: where German writes Bisulfid, English writes bisulfide. This atlas maps that boundary across the full sulfide compound family and extends into adjacent compound classes.

What This Atlas Maps

The atlas covers four interconnected areas:

  • Compound classes — sulfide, bisulfide, disulfide, polysulfide, and related classes with their German and English names
  • Language boundary — the systematic -id/-ide suffix pattern across German inorganic nomenclature
  • Specific compounds — individual compounds like sodium bisulfide, pyrite, and molybdenum disulfide
  • Disambiguation nodes — boundaries with bisulfite, sulfate, and other sulfur compound classes that are commonly confused with sulfides

The Central Axis: Bisulfid and the Missing E

bisulfid.com is named after the German form of bisulfide — Bisulfid. The domain captures the German spelling, which reflects a documented convention: German chemical names for many ionic compound classes drop the final E that appears in English. This is not a typo or regional error — it is a systematic feature of German inorganic nomenclature.

The axis runs through the entire compound family: Bisulfid/bisulfide, Sulfid/sulfide, Disulfid/disulfide, Oxid/oxide, Chlorid/chloride, and many others. See The Missing E for the full pattern.
