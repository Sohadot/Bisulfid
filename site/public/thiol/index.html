Thiol

Thiols are organic compounds containing a sulfur–hydrogen (S–H) group bonded to a carbon chain or ring: general formula R–SH. They are sometimes called mercaptans. Thiols are the organic analog of hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S), in the same way that alcohols (R–OH) are the organic analog of water (H&sub2;O). bisulfid.com covers thiols as a boundary node at the edge of the inorganic sulfide family.

Thiols and the Bisulfide Boundary

The HS¹¯ ion (bisulfide) is the deprotonated form of H&sub2;S. When a thiol R–SH loses its proton, it gives a thiolate ion R–S¹¯. Thiolate ions are the organic analogs of the bisulfide ion: both are R/H–S¹¯ species, but one is organic (thiolate) and one is inorganic (bisulfide/hydrosulfide).

This adjacency makes thiols a useful boundary node for the atlas. They help define where the inorganic bisulfide/sulfide chemistry ends and organic sulfur chemistry begins.

What This Atlas Does Not Cover

The Bisulfid Atlas does not cover thiols in detail. Thiol chemistry is organic chemistry. bisulfid.com focuses on inorganic sulfide and bisulfide compound classes. Thiols appear here only to mark the organic boundary of the HS¹¯ / S²¯ family.

Disulfide Bonds from Thiols

Two thiol groups (R–SH) can oxidize to form a disulfide bond (R–S–S–R). This is the organic disulfide, distinct from ionic inorganic disulfides. See Disulfide Bonds for the biochemical context.
