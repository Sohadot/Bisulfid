Iron Sulfide

Iron sulfide is a family of minerals and compounds in which iron bonds with sulfur. The two most important members are pyrrhotite (FeS, iron(II) sulfide) and pyrite (FeS&sub2;, iron disulfide). German: Eisensulfid (for the compound class), Pyrrhotin (for pyrrhotite), Pyrit (for pyrite).

Pyrrhotite: FeS

Pyrrhotite (FeS) is iron(II) sulfide — one iron atom per sulfide ion (S²¯). It is a mineral belonging to the sulfide compound class. Pyrrhotite has magnetic properties and occurs in mafic igneous rocks. Its composition is sometimes written as Fe⊂1−xS (with iron vacancies), giving a range of compositions rather than a strictly stoichiometric formula.

Pyrite: FeS₂

Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) is iron disulfide — it contains a disulfide anion (S&sub2;²¯) rather than a simple sulfide ion. Pyrite is the most abundant sulfide mineral in the Earth’s crust. Its golden metallic luster has earned it the nickname “fool’s gold.” Despite its formula, pyrite belongs to the disulfide compound class, not the simple sulfide class.

Iron Sulfides in the Compound Class Map

The iron sulfide family illustrates the distinction between the sulfide and disulfide compound classes:

  • FeS (pyrrhotite) — iron(II) sulfide; contains S²¯
  • FeS&sub2; (pyrite) — iron disulfide; contains S&sub2;²¯

Both are named “iron sulfide” in casual usage, but they are chemically distinct.
