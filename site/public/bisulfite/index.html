Bisulfite

Bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯) is a sulfite compound. It is completely different from bisulfide (HS¹¯). bisulfid.com is a bisulfide reference site, not a bisulfite site. This page exists to draw the boundary precisely.

What Bisulfite Is

Bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯) is the hydrogen sulfite anion. It contains sulfur bonded to three oxygen atoms, plus one hydrogen. The sulfur in bisulfite is in the +4 oxidation state. Bisulfite is the partially deprotonated form of sulfurous acid (H&sub2;SO&sub3;).

Common bisulfite compounds include sodium bisulfite (NaHSO&sub3;) and potassium bisulfite (KHSO&sub3;). These are used as food preservatives and in various industrial processes.

What Bisulfide Is (Not Bisulfite)

Bisulfide (HS¹¯) is the hydrogen sulfide anion. It contains only sulfur and hydrogen — no oxygen. The sulfur in bisulfide is in the −2 oxidation state. Bisulfide is the partially deprotonated form of hydrogen sulfide (H&sub2;S).

The two compounds share the prefix bi- but refer to completely different chemistry. They are not related to each other beyond sharing sulfur as an element.

The German Forms Are Also Different

In German nomenclature:

  • Bisulfid (German) = bisulfide (English): the HS¹¯ compound class. Uses -id suffix.
  • Bisulfit (German) = bisulfite (English): the HSO&sub3;¹¯ compound class. Uses -it suffix.

The German suffixes are different: -id (bisulfid/bisulfide) vs -it (bisulfit/bisulfite). This is not the same -id/-ide pattern as the rest of the German naming system — the -it suffix follows a different convention for sulfite-class compounds.

bisulfid.com is Not a Bisulfite Site

bisulfid.com was built at the German-English boundary of the bisulfide compound class. The domain name reflects Bisulfid (the German form of bisulfide), not Bisulfit. This atlas covers the sulfide/bisulfide family. Bisulfite is referenced here only as a disambiguation node.
