Language Boundary

German chemical nomenclature uses a systematic suffix pattern that differs from English. Where English writes bisulfide, German writes Bisulfid. This is not an error or a regional variant — it is a documented convention that applies across the sulfide compound family and beyond. bisulfid.com was built at this boundary.

The Two Languages at the Boundary

DE Deutsch

German chemical naming uses the -id suffix for this compound class. Bisulfid, Sulfid, Hydrosulfid, Disulfid, Oxid, Chlorid. The domain name bisulfid.com is the German form — the identity layer of this atlas.

EN English

English chemical naming uses the -ide suffix. Bisulfide, sulfide, hydrosulfide, disulfide, oxide, chloride. The English form is the more common usage in global search and most industrial documentation.

The Systematic Pattern

The -id/-ide difference is not isolated to bisulfid/bisulfide. It is a systematic pattern across many inorganic compound classes:

German (DE) English (EN) Compound class
Bisulfid Bisulfide Hydrogen sulfide anion compounds (HS¹¯)
Sulfid Sulfide Sulfur anion compounds (S²¯)
Hydrosulfid Hydrosulfide Alternative name for HS¹¯ compounds
Disulfid Disulfide S–S bond compounds
Oxid Oxide Oxygen anion compounds
Chlorid Chloride Chlorine anion compounds

Why This Boundary Is Architecturally Significant

The -id/-ide boundary is not merely orthographic. It has consequences for:

  • Search intent — Users searching in German or from German-language industrial contexts may use the -id form. The domain captures this search boundary.
  • Multilingual reference architecture — A reference surface that holds the German form can map the full compound family across both naming conventions without privileging either.
  • Disambiguation — The boundary makes the distinction between bisulfide (HS¹¯ compounds) and bisulfite (HSO₃¹¯ compounds) more visible by anchoring to a specific naming tradition.

Bisulfite Is Not on This Side of the Boundary

Bisulfite (HSO₃¹¯) is a distinct compound class from bisulfide. It appears in this atlas only as a disambiguation node: to name the boundary precisely and confirm that bisulfid.com is not a bisulfite sequencing site or a generic sulfite chemistry reference. The German form of bisulfite is Bisulfit — also distinct.
