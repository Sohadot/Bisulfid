Sulfur Uses

Sulfur compounds appear in a wide range of natural and industrial contexts. This page provides context for where the compound classes covered in the Bisulfid Atlas are relevant — without making specific market claims, procurement claims, or industrial performance claims.

Natural Occurrence

Sulfide compounds are among the most common mineral types in the Earth’s crust. Major sulfide mineral occurrences:

  • Pyrite (FeS&sub2;) — iron disulfide, the most abundant sulfide mineral globally
  • Sphalerite (ZnS) — the primary zinc ore mineral
  • Galena (PbS) — the primary lead ore mineral
  • Pyrrhotite (FeS) — iron sulfide mineral in mafic rocks

Industrial Sulfide Compounds

Several sulfide-class compounds are produced and used industrially:

  • Sodium bisulfide (NaHS) — used in the pulp and paper industry, mining, and water treatment
  • Sodium sulfide (Na&sub2;S) — used in leather processing and chemical synthesis
  • Molybdenum disulfide (MoS&sub2;) — used as a solid lubricant and in industrial coatings
  • Zinc sulfide (ZnS) — used in optical materials and phosphors

Disulfide Bonds in Biology

Disulfide bonds (S–S bonds) form between cysteine amino acids in proteins, helping maintain protein three-dimensional structure. This is an important biochemical context for the disulfide compound class, though the Bisulfid Atlas covers this only as a reference node rather than in depth.
