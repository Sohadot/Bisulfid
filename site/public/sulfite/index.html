Sulfite

Sulfite (SO&sub3;²¯) is a sulfur-oxygen compound class. It is completely different from sulfide (S²¯). The German name for sulfite is Sulfit — distinct from Sulfid (sulfide). This page draws the boundary between the sulfite and sulfide families.

What Sulfite Is

The sulfite ion (SO&sub3;²¯) consists of one sulfur atom bonded to three oxygen atoms, carrying a charge of −2. Sulfur in sulfite is in the +4 oxidation state. Sulfite is the fully deprotonated form of sulfurous acid (H&sub2;SO&sub3;). Common sulfite compounds include sodium sulfite (Na&sub2;SO&sub3;) and potassium sulfite (K&sub2;SO&sub3;).

Sulfite vs Sulfide: The Core Difference

The two compound classes differ fundamentally in composition and chemistry:

  • Sulfide (S²¯): sulfur only, oxidation state −2. No oxygen. Examples: Na&sub2;S, FeS.
  • Sulfite (SO&sub3;²¯): sulfur plus three oxygens, oxidation state +4. Examples: Na&sub2;SO&sub3;.

The similar-sounding names (sulfide / sulfite) are a persistent source of confusion. They are not the same compound class, not interchangeable, and not related in use or chemistry.

German Names: Sulfid vs Sulfit

The German naming reinforces the distinction:

  • Sulfid (German) = sulfide (English): S²¯ compounds. The -id suffix.
  • Sulfit (German) = sulfite (English): SO&sub3;²¯ compounds. The -it suffix.

The -it suffix in Sulfit is a different convention from the -id suffix in Sulfid. This aligns with how German handles oxyanion compound names more broadly. See Terminology for the full convention map.

Bisulfite

The half-protonated form of sulfite is bisulfite (HSO&sub3;¹¯). Bisulfite is also covered as a disambiguation node in this atlas, because its name is often confused with bisulfide (HS¹¯).
