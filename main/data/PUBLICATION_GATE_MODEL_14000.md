# Publication Gate Model — 14,000-Page Launch Foundation (Sprint 6M-G)

## Gate definitions

| Gate | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Visibility** | Controlled public HTML may exist under `site/public/` when generated by `--render-public-launch-foundation`. Visibility does not change route registry status. |
| **Indexation** | Search engines may crawl/index pages (`indexable: true`, `robots: index,follow`). Remains **CLOSED** in this sprint. |
| **Sitemap** | URLs appear in generated sitemap artifacts and `in_sitemap: true`. Remains **CLOSED** in this sprint. |
| **Navigation** | Routes appear in site navigation and `in_navigation: true`. Remains **CLOSED** in this sprint. |
| **Source approval** | Authority evidence verified and bound in `source_registry.json`. Remains **CLOSED** — no registry mutation. |
| **Claim approval** | Terminology/science claims approved in claim registries. Remains **CLOSED** — no registry mutation. |

## Gates opened in Sprint 6M-G

- **Visibility gate: OPEN** — 14,000 pages rendered to `site/public/` via explicit build command.
- All other gates remain **CLOSED**.

## Gates remaining closed

- Indexation (`noindex,nofollow` on all public foundation pages; `indexable: false` on all routes)
- Sitemap (no sitemap output; `in_sitemap: false` on all routes)
- Navigation (no nav emission; `in_navigation: false` on all routes)
- Source approval (registry inactive; `[SOURCE REQUIRED]` preserved)
- Claim approval (`no_claims_approved` posture on all pages)

## Why visibility can precede full indexation

Public visibility allows controlled human and engineering review of rendered output, CDN/hosting wiring, and governance truthfulness before search exposure. Indexation requires separate authorization, robots policy review, and sitemap planning.

## Why sitemap exposure must be controlled

Blind sitemap publication would signal indexation intent for routes that are not source-approved, not claim-approved, and not publication-ready. Sitemap inclusion is a distinct gate validated against `sitemap_policy.json`.

## Why navigation exposure must be controlled

Navigation implies editorial curation and user-facing wayfinding authority. Mass navigation of 14,000 planned routes would misrepresent corpus maturity and bypass claim/source boundaries.

## Source-required pages

Pages with unresolved authority retain `[SOURCE REQUIRED]` markers in content and visible source posture in HTML. Public visibility does **not** remove or soften these markers.

## Claim boundaries

All pages render with `claim-approval-state: no_claims_approved`. No page implies that terminology or science claims are approved because they are publicly visible.

## Public visibility ≠ source approval

Rendering HTML outside quarantine is governed visibility only. The source registry remains inactive and unmodified. Pages explicitly state that source approval is not implied.

## Public visibility ≠ claim approval

Claim registries remain locked. Public foundation banners and metadata state that claim approval is not implied.
