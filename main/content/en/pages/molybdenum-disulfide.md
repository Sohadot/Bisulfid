## Chemical identity

The formula of molybdenum disulfide is MoS2, as recorded independently by the PubChem and NIST chemistry databases.

The formula states composition only: one molybdenum to two sulfur. It says nothing about how the atoms are arranged in the solid, and the database field name "MolecularFormula" should not be read as implying that MoS2 forms discrete molecules.

## Bulk structure: stacked S–Mo–S layers

Bulk MoS2 is a layered material in which each layer is a three-atom-thick S–Mo–S stack.

This description belongs to the bulk crystal. Single layers and few-layer flakes are separate forms whose properties this reference does not cover.

## Three polytypes: 1T, 2H and 3R

The reviewed literature distinguishes three polytypes of MoS2, labelled 1T, 2H and 3R, which differ in the coordination of molybdenum and in the stacking of the layers: 2H and 3R share trigonal-prismatic coordination of molybdenum but stack differently.

A structural value for MoS2 is therefore incomplete unless it names the polytype it belongs to. This reference covers 2H; 1T is out of scope, and 3R is recorded only as a boundary.

The numeral in a polytype label identifies the number of S–Mo–S layers in the crystallographic unit cell; T, H and R denote trigonal, hexagonal and rhombohedral symmetry in the reviewed convention. These labels describe structure, not a different MoS2 stoichiometry, so a formula alone cannot identify the stacking arrangement.

## The 2H polytype

In the 2H polytype, bulk MoS2 adopts the hexagonal space group P6₃/mmc (No. 194), with a c-axis repeat of 12.30(7) Å in the 1923 determination and 12.295 Å in the 1983 determination.

The two determinations come from different originating studies: Dickinson and Pauling's 1923 X-ray study of the mineral molybdenite, and the 1983 single-crystal study by Schönfeld, Huang and Moss. Both crystallographic records are retrieved from the Crystallography Open Database; the database is the retrieval route, not the source of the science.

The 2015 RSC Advances review by Song, Park and Choi summarises the same 2H structure in its Table 1: the same hexagonal space group, the layer stacking sequence AbA BaB, and rounded lattice values a = 3.15 Å and c = 12.30 Å, which the review takes from earlier literature (its reference 35). A review table is a literature summary, not a further measurement: it supports the structural description but is not counted as a third determination alongside the 1923 and 1983 studies.

The c-axis values 12.30(7) Å and 12.295 Å describe the repeat of the bulk 2H crystallographic unit cell along c. They should not be quoted as measurements of the thickness of one S–Mo–S sandwich: a lattice repeat and an individual structural layer are different quantities.

## Where the name 2H comes from

Dickinson and Pauling described molybdenite as hexagonal in 1923 but did not use the label 2H; the explicit 2H and 3R terminology appears in the 1983 study, whose title refers to single crystals of 2H- and 3R-MoS2.

The label was already in use two years earlier within the same research lineage. J. J. Huang's 1981 master's thesis at the University of Houston refers to molybdenite as 2H-MoS2, describes it as hexagonal with A-B-A stacking of MoS2 layers, and credits Dickinson and Pauling with the first investigation of the structure. Huang is a co-author of the 1983 study, and a thesis is not peer-reviewed, so the thesis documents how the label was used; it is not an independent confirmation of the 1983 results. Neither source is presented here as the first use of the 2H label.

## Lattice parameter a: two coexisting determinations

Two independent determinations report the in-plane lattice parameter a of bulk 2H-MoS2 as 3.15(2) Å (Dickinson and Pauling, 1923) and 3.161 Å (Schönfeld, Huang and Moss, 1983).

The values agree within the stated uncertainty of the 1923 value and are kept as separate determinations rather than averaged into one number. Both apply to bulk 2H only; a value for a single layer or for 3R must not be substituted for them.

The crystallographic notation 3.15(2) Å expresses 3.15 Å with a reported uncertainty of 0.02 Å in the final digits. Its corresponding interval, 3.13–3.17 Å, includes the later 3.161 Å value. This is evidence of compatibility between the two cited bulk 2H determinations, not a warrant to assign either number universally to every MoS2 sample or polytype.

| Determination | Reported a | Method | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dickinson & Pauling (1923) | 3.15(2) Å | X-ray crystal-structure determination (1923) | 2H-MoS2 (molybdenite), bulk single crystal |
| Schönfeld, Huang & Moss (1983) | 3.161 Å | single-crystal X-ray diffraction (1983) | 2H-MoS2, bulk single crystal |

## Boundary: 2H is not 3R

The 3R polytype of MoS2 is a distinct structure with space group R3m, layer stacking AbA BcB CaC, and reviewed lattice parameters a = 3.17 Å and c = 18.38 Å, so its values must not be attributed to the 2H form.

## What the sources support together, what stays open, and what not to infer

Each statement below draws only on the evidence cited in brackets. It separates the primary crystallographic records, the review that summarises them and the supporting thesis, because they carry different weight.

### Supported by the cited sources

- Bulk 2H-MoS2 is hexagonal: the 1923 and 1983 determinations both record space group P6₃/mmc (No. 194), and the 2015 review lists the same structure in its literature summary.
- The 1923 and 1983 values of the in-plane lattice parameter a agree within the uncertainty stated for the 1923 value.

### Open: the governed evidence does not settle it

- The band gap and the electronic and optical behaviour of any form of MoS2. No cited record is admitted for them, and the review used here is admitted for structure only, so this reference gives no value or character for them.
- Density, lubricant behaviour and other material properties. They lie outside the scope of every record cited here.
- The properties of single layers and few-layer MoS2. The structure described here is that of bulk crystals, and none of the cited records is admitted for monolayer measurements.
- The lattice parameters of the 3R polytype as primary facts. The review's 3R values are held only as a boundary; no primary 3R determination has been acquired.

### Not to be inferred from the cited records

- That the review table and the thesis are further measurements of the 2H lattice. Both report values from earlier work, and the thesis shares an author with the 1983 study.
- That the 2H values apply to the 3R or 1T polytypes, or to single layers. Each value here belongs to bulk 2H.
- That the c-axis repeat is the thickness of one S–Mo–S layer. It is the repeat of the bulk 2H unit cell.

## What this reference does not establish
